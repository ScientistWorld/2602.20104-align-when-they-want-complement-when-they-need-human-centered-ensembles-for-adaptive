## What the gym is now

The hidden test is an extended College Admission world, generated by `eval/test/world.py` and present only in `eval/test/`:
- **Groups.** There are two applicant groups, with the paper's score-dominant and GPA-dominant outcome rules. The outcome adds context, interaction and non-linear terms in the group where humans defer.
- **Group membership.** It follows a partly non-linear function of six context features, so it can only be inferred approximately.
- **Confident evaluators.** In one group they apply one linear rule with a confidently held bias, plus random slips.
- **Unsure evaluators.** In the other group they are unsure and at chance.
- **Thresholds.** τ ~ U[0.6, 0.8] is drawn per decision, as in the paper's behaviour-grounded protocol.

There are four experiments: group known or hidden, crossed with reliable (α ≈ 0.90) or less reliable (α ≈ 0.75) evaluators. Each has 7,000 train rows, 3,000 visible dev rows and 20,000 hidden test rows.
- The agent sees features (plus `group` in the known-group experiments) for the test rows.
- It also sees `label`, `human_pred` and `human_conf` for the train and dev rows.
- The test-row labels, human judgements and confidences live only in `eval/test/answers/`.

`team_accuracy` is computed exactly over τ and the reliance coin, so a seed cannot shift it. The paper's own two-feature College Admission sweep is still reproduced as an unscored check. It matches the authors' released numbers exactly (for example α = 0.9: adaptive 0.8060, behaviour-aware 0.7660, complementary 0.7783).

The reproduced method is the paper's adaptive ensemble at the paper's synthetic scale:
- logistic regression throughout;
- specialists with Proposition 5 weights;
- Eq. 7 routing on the known group, or on a learned estimate of P(C^h > τ | x) when the group is hidden. This matches the authors' WoofNette pipeline, which routes on a learned human-confidence model.

The paper's claim holds in the paper's direction in all four experiments. The adaptive ensemble beats every single-model baseline on team accuracy by +0.06 to +0.11:

| experiment | adaptive (reference) | standard | behaviour-aware | aligned | complementary |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| group hidden | **0.8904** | 0.8029 | 0.8042 | 0.7307 | 0.8315 |
| group hidden, low reliability | **0.7863** | 0.7175 | 0.7300 | 0.6505 | 0.7461 |
| group known | **0.9174** | 0.8054 | 0.8079 | 0.7364 | 0.8298 |
| group known, low reliability | **0.8062** | 0.7151 | 0.7316 | 0.6497 | 0.7459 |

This effect is about 60× the seed spread (sd 0.0010).

## 3. Shortcuts and weaknesses left, and why

- **Dev answers are visible.** This is by design: dev is a development split.
  - Copying dev labels (`sc_copy_dev_labels`) gains +0.018 on the dev slice and 0.0000 on the hidden test.
  - Training or tuning on dev is allowed and honest.
- **A single flexible "behaviour-aware" model is close to the reference (`gbm_behavior_aware`).** This is the gym's behaviour-aware baseline with gradient boosting instead of logistic regression.
  - It gains +0.004 to +0.006 team accuracy in every experiment but loses 0.012 to 0.020 trust. A flexible single model can learn the routing implicitly; the paper's tradeoff (Theorem 2) binds for a fixed parametric model class.
  - After this iteration's coefficient change it scores **−0.0022** (SE 0.0009, P(gain > 0) = 0.00), up from a tie at −0.0008.
  - Within the same learner class the paper's structure still wins: `gbm_specialists` scores +0.0058 against −0.0022.
  - I left this tradeoff in place. It is an honest alternative, not a loophole.
- **The real rank divides each metric by its spread across future attempts, which is unknown here.** `run_checks.py` now brackets it with two stand-in pools (last table of `redteam/v2/summary.md`):
  - **Pool of competitive attempts only.** Trust varies about twice as much as team accuracy. `gbm_behavior_aware` then comes out slightly above the reference (+0.28 normalized units). Genuinely better variants score +0.9 to +2.2.
  - **Pool of all honest attempts, weak ones included.** Trust is up-weighted. `gbm_behavior_aware` is clearly below (−0.28). But `gbm_specialists` (−0.06) and `gbm_team` (−0.17) also fall below the reference: they trade 0.01–0.03 trust in the known-group experiments for team accuracy, because gradient boosting fits the evaluators' linear rule less exactly than logistic regression.
  - **Variants better on both team accuracy and trust** (`hybrid_comp_gbm_router_gbm`, `proxy_gbm`, `hybrid_comp_gbm`, `ref_x3data`) score above the reference in every version.
  - How tradeoffs rank depends on that normalization. I cannot fix this from inside the gym.
- **`redteam/` documents the better variants.** If `redteam/v2/variants.py` or this file is shipped to future agents, they can copy `hybrid_comp_gbm_router_gbm` (+0.0155) without contributing anything. Whether these files are visible is a packaging decision outside the gym.
- **The paper's "RRS ≈ oracle" claim does not reproduce here.** In this world RRS routes poorly: −0.0954 team accuracy with the group hidden and reliable evaluators. This is a finding about where the paper's practical router falls short. The reference uses the paper's Eq. 7 with a learned confidence model, as in the authors' WoofNette pipeline, and RRS stays available as `--router rrs`.
- **The WoofNette image benchmark is not reproduced.** The milestone stays `secondary_claims`.
- **`validate.py` warns** that `eval/test/world.py` and `eval/paper_reproduction_eval.py` "look detached from the method". Both are generators by design: one builds the hidden world, the other simulates the paper's own CGPR draw protocol for the unscored check. Neither scores ranked method outputs.

## 5. Headroom check

**Bound** is the Bayes policy from the hidden generator, minus the reference.

| metric | experiment | reference | bound | at bound? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| team_accuracy | hidden / hidden-low / known / known-low | 0.8904 / 0.7863 / 0.9174 / 0.8062 | +0.0333 / +0.0363 / +0.0218 / +0.0242 | no, in every experiment |
| reliance_probability | hidden / hidden-low | 0.9386 / 0.7631 | `proxy_gbm` and `hybrid_comp_gbm_router_gbm` gain +0.015 to +0.017 while also gaining team accuracy | no |
| reliance_probability | known / known-low | 0.9815 / 0.7970 | features-only limit ≈ 1 − slip ≈ 0.985 / 0.80 | **yes**, within about 0.005: the evaluators' rule is linear and the paper's aligned specialist recovers it |

- **What was added for headroom.** Earlier rounds of this iteration added a non-linear outcome where humans defer, a group that the features only partly reveal, and confidently biased evaluators. Nothing was added in this final round; the headroom was already there.
- **Simple and hand-written comparisons sit below the reference:**
  - gradient-boosted standard AI: −0.0129;
  - gradient-boosted behaviour-aware model: −0.0022;
  - the paper's hand-coded rules: −0.053 and −0.068;
  - mimicking the human: −0.174;
  - the gym's frozen baselines: −0.12 to −0.16.
- **Brute force and private search.** The paper's method exists to avoid needing the human's private state (confidence) at test time, and that is never available here. The objective on test rows needs the hidden labels, judgements and confidences, so neither enumeration nor private search can compute it. On dev the objective is computable, but dev is not scored.

## 6. Weights and coefficients

- **Experiment weights are unchanged:** group hidden 0.35, group known 0.25, each low-reliability variant 0.20. The hidden-group setting is the deployment setting the paper's practical method targets. The known-group setting is the paper's synthetic default.
- **`reliance_probability` coefficient: 0.4 → 0.5** in all four experiments. `team_accuracy` stays at 1.0; the diagnostics stay at 0.
  - **Why:** trust is half of the paper's thesis ("align when they want"). At 0.4 a single flexible behaviour-aware model, which buys 0.005 team accuracy with 0.014 trust, tied the reference (−0.0008, about 1 SE). At 0.5 it is about 2.5 SE below (−0.0022).
  - **Effect on better variants:** they all stay above noise (+0.0043 to +0.0155, up from +0.0055 to +0.0147 at 0.4).
  - **Team accuracy still dominates:** a method that fails the task cannot outrank one that solves it through trust alone. For example, mimicking the human everywhere scores −0.174.
  - The before/after tables are `redteam/v2/summary_round3_coef0.4.md` and `redteam/v2/summary.md`.

## 7. Rounds within this iteration

- **Round 1** (`summary_round1.md`): evaluator overhaul. The world had a human red-flag rule. A gradient-boosted model that ignores humans outranked the reference (+0.0121), so the world was redesigned.
- **Round 2** (`summary_round2.md`): confident linear human bias. Shortcuts were closed and simple models fell below the reference. Headroom was too thin: the best honest variant reached +0.0022.
- **Round 3** (`summary_round3_coef0.4.md`): non-linear outcome where humans defer and a wider group gate, chosen with the world-design sweep in `redteam/v2/design_sweep.md`. Better variants reached +0.0055 to +0.0147, and the reliance coefficient was set to 0.4.
- **Round 4** (this report):
  - reliance coefficient 0.5;
  - a spread-normalized view added to `run_checks.py`;
  - the authors' release is no longer tracked in git; `scripts/download.sh` clones it at commit `28892a0` (tested from an empty layout, and idempotent);
  - the pre-hardening `VERIFICATION.md`, `PROGRESS.md` and `DESIGN_NOTES.md` are marked superseded, and `redteam/README.md` says which folders target the old gym;
  - the final end-to-end job.
