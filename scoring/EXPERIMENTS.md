# Experiment Overview

All four experiments score AI recommendations by the expected accuracy of
the final human decision under confidence-gated, probabilistic reliance.
Trust is the constraint: how often the AI agrees with the human where the
human feels sure. Each experiment is a separate draw of applicants and
evaluators from the same admissions population.

## admission_group_known

This is the main setting with reliable evaluators: where they feel sure,
they are right about 90% of the time. The applicant's group is given at
test time.

It asks whether an assistant can raise team accuracy on the cases where
humans are unsure and defer, without losing the trust it earns on the cases
where they feel sure.

## admission_group_hidden

Same population and evaluators, but the group is not given at test time. The
assistant sees only applicant features. It never sees the human's
confidence, judgement or threshold.

It asks whether the assistant can still tell, from the applicant alone, when
to earn trust and when to correct the human. This is the deployment-like
setting.

## admission_group_known_low_reliability / admission_group_hidden_low_reliability

The same two settings with less reliable evaluators: where they feel sure,
they are right about 75% of the time, and they are less often sure.

These check that the measurement stays meaningful when human competence
changes. A weaker trust signal changes how much there is to gain from
matching the human versus correcting them.

## Not scored: paper reproduction

`scoring/paper_reproduction.json` holds the paper's original two-feature
College Admission sweep over human reliability. It is regenerated by
`scripts/reproduce.sh` and compared with the paper's released numbers. It is
not part of the rank.
